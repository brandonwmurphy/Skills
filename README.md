SKILL README
Dalio Meeting Facilitator

Skill Name	dalio-meeting-facilitator
Version	1.0
Author	Brandon Murphy | Bridgestone TMO
Framework	Ray Dalio — Principles (Radical Transparency)
Best Used For	Pre-meeting prep, group decisions, structured facilitation
Not For	Solo decisions, interpersonal conflict resolution

Overview
This skill turns Claude into a meeting preparation coach grounded in Ray Dalio's Principles, specifically radical transparency and believability-weighted decision making. It helps you design and run meetings where disagreements surface early, opinions are weighted by track record, and decisions are documented with clear reasoning.

The skill operates as a structured coaching conversation, one question at a time, adapting to your answers. It does not run the meeting for you. It prepares you to run it well.

When to Use This Skill
Trigger this skill when any of the following apply:

•	You are preparing to run a team meeting where a real decision needs to be made
•	You need to facilitate a group discussion where multiple people have strong or conflicting opinions
•	You are heading into a leadership meeting and want to structure the agenda around outcomes, not discussion
•	You need to resolve a team disagreement and want a principled framework to guide it
•	You want to ensure that the most qualified voices carry the most weight in a decision

Do not use this skill for:

•	Solo decisions with no group component (use a dedicated decision-coaching approach instead)
•	Deep interpersonal conflicts rooted in trust or relationship issues
•	Purely informational meetings with no decision required

How to Trigger the Skill
Say any of the following to activate the skill:

“I need to prep for a team meeting on [topic]”
“Help me facilitate a group decision about [X]”
“I have a leadership meeting and need to structure the discussion”
“I need to resolve a team disagreement on [topic]”

You can also provide context upfront to accelerate the prep. For example: share the meeting purpose, the attendees, the time constraint, and any existing documents or notes. The more context you provide, the faster the skill reaches the useful part.

The Five Steps
The skill walks through five steps in sequence, one question at a time. Steps 1 through 4 happen before the meeting. Step 5 happens after.

01
Before meeting	Define the Decision
Sharpens a vague topic into one concrete, answerable question the team needs to walk out with resolved. If multiple decisions exist, they are ranked and scoped to one per meeting segment.
•	Output: A single, clearly framed decision question
•	Watch for: Vague goals like ‘discuss strategy’ that are not actually decisions
•	Dalio principle: If you can’t state the decision clearly, you’re not ready to meet

02
Before meeting	Map the Participants
Identifies who is in the room and calibrates how much weight each person’s opinion should carry based on their track record with this type of problem.
•	Output: A believability map, role (decision-maker, advisor, observer), and known positions
•	Watch for: Defaulting to seniority over track record
•	Dalio principle: Not all opinions are equal. Weight them by demonstrated expertise on this specific problem type

03
Before meeting	Design the Agenda
Structures the decision block into three segments: frame, surface disagreements, decide and document. Target is 20 minutes or less for the decision portion.
•	Frame (5 min): State the decision, share context and data, name believability weights
•	Surface disagreements (10 min): Each participant states position and reasoning. Silence = agreement.
•	Decide and document (5 min): Decision-maker makes the call. Reasoning is documented, not just outcome.

04
Before meeting	Prepare for Conflict
Identifies where disagreement will surface and prepares you to handle it. Fast consensus is treated as a warning sign, not a green light.
•	Output: A conflict map with the likely disagreement, what data would resolve it, and what each person would need to change their mind
•	Classify disagreements: Facts (resolvable with data), Values (requires judgment call), Priorities (requires decision-maker call)
•	Dalio principle: If everyone agrees too quickly, someone isn’t thinking

05
After meeting	Document the Decision
Builds a decision record that captures not just what was decided but why. Decisions without documented reasoning cannot be learned from or audited later.
•	Decision: What was decided
•	Reasoning: Why, not just what
•	Dissenting views: Who disagreed and their argument
•	Believability assessment: Whose input carried the most weight and why
•	Next actions: Owner and timing for each
•	Revisit trigger: What would cause this decision to be reconsidered

Tips for Getting the Most Out of the Skill

Provide Granola context	Share recent meeting notes or transcripts. The skill uses existing context to skip redundant questions and focus on gaps.
Name your attendees upfront	Include each person’s role and, if known, their track record on this type of problem. This enables a meaningful believability weighting.
State your time constraint	The agenda design changes significantly between a 20-minute slot and a 90-minute working session.
Push back if it feels too smooth	If the prep feels easy and everyone seems aligned, flag it. The skill is designed to surface the disagreement you have not seen yet.
Use Step 5 even for fast decisions	The decision record is most valuable for recurring decisions or ones that are likely to be revisited. Build the habit.
Combine with meeting recaps	After the meeting, bring the notes back to Claude to complete Step 5 and generate the decision record.

Boundaries and Limitations
•	Prepares the facilitator. Does not run the meeting or speak on your behalf.
•	Designed for group decisions. For solo decisions, use a dedicated decision-coaching approach.
•	Does not resolve interpersonal conflicts rooted in trust or relationship breakdowns.
•	Believability weighting is a framework, not an algorithm. The skill helps you reason through it, but you make the call.
•	The skill works one question at a time by design. If you want to skip ahead, you can, but you may miss a step that matters.
